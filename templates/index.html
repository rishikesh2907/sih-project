Vitals that keep reporting after the network drops.

VitalEdge is a rugged wrist-worn monitor built for flood camps, mass-casualty triage and clinics that can't count on steady power or signal. It streams six physiological signals into a 60-second rolling window, feeds an LSTM autoencoder for anomaly detection, and flags a responder the moment the pattern drifts from baseline.

      What's actually on the wrist

      Every part below exists as a modelled component on the device to the left — this is the legend, not a marketing diagram.

      ▣

      Touchscreen display

      Sunlight-readable panel behind sealed glass, showing HR, SpO2, temperature and status at a glance.

      !

      SOS button

      Recessed red button on the right edge. A long press pushes a manual distress signal regardless of sensor state.

      ●

      Secondary button

      Cycles screen modes and confirms actions — mutes an alert or wakes the display without touching glass in wet or gloved conditions.

      ◎

      Rear PPG sensor array

      Multi-LED optical assembly on the case back reads heart rate and SpO2, and its waveform envelope is where the respiration estimate comes from.

      ✦

      Optical LED cluster

      Green, red and infrared emitters paired with a photodiode, tuned for varied skin tones and low-light field use.

      °

      Skin-temperature sensor

      Dedicated contact thermistor next to the optical array, separate from the PPG reading for cleaner data.

      ⎓

      Charging contacts

      Four gold pogo-pin pads on the case back mate with a magnetic dock — no port to seal or corrode.

      ⚙

      MCU, battery & radio

      Internal PCB carries the ESP32, a six-channel acquisition front end for EDA and ECG at the strap contacts, and the wireless module — visible in the exploded view.

      How a reading becomes an alert

      Every step below except the last runs inside Flask. This page never computes an anomaly score — it only polls and displays what the backend decides.

      01

      Wearable / sensor stream

      The simulated wearable generates one six-feature sensor sample every second and sends it to the Flask backend.

      →
      02

      Flask backend

      Ingests the stream and owns the rolling 60-second window — the browser keeps no buffer of its own.

      →
      03

      6-feature preprocessing

      Assembles the window into the exact order the model expects: HR/BVP, ACC, TEMP, RESP, EDA, ECG.

      →
      04

      ONNX LSTM autoencoder

      VitalEdgeDeepAutoencoder (vitaledge_6d_deep_autoencoder.onnx) reconstructs the sequence — 2 LSTM layers, hidden size 64, input (1, 60, 6).

      →
      05

      Reconstruction MSE → threshold

      MSE above 1.78 resolves to ABNORMAL; at or below it, NORMAL.

      →
      06 · this page

      Dashboard + 3D watch

      Receives the inference result from POST /api/data and renders the verdict on the graphs, alert panel and watch screen.

      The frontend has no independent ML path. It generates one sensor sample every second and sends it to POST /api/data. Flask owns the rolling 60-second window, runs the ONNX autoencoder and calculates the reconstruction MSE. The returned result is passed to updateFromBackend(), which updates the dashboard, graphs and watch screen. Simulate abnormality and Resume normal call POST /api/simulate-abnormal and POST /api/reset on the backend directly.

      Where it's meant to be worn

      FLOOD & CYCLONE CAMPS

      Relief camps with patchy power

      Long battery life and a screen that reads NORMAL or ALERT without a phone nearby, for volunteers checking dozens of people a shift.

      MASS-CASUALTY TRIAGE

      First minutes after an incident

      One glance at HR, SpO2 and temperature helps a responder decide who needs attention first, before a full assessment is possible.

      REMOTE CLINICS

      Low-connectivity monitoring

      Local risk scoring on the ESP32 means an alert still fires even if the backend link is down, then syncs once connectivity returns.
